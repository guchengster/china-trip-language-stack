The China-trip language stack: a practical comparison matrix

Updated November 2026 · ~9 min read

Disclosure: FlyTalk (the app in the last row) is built by us. Everything below is verifiable in the stores; where a claim depends on the network, it's marked as such.

Most "best Chinese apps" lists compare apps by category. That's the wrong axis for a short trip. The useful axis is which failure each app prevents:

  1. I can't say it. → speaking tools
  2. I can't understand the answer. → listening and reply-training tools
  3. I can't read it. → translation and OCR tools
  4. I can't pay / get there. → logistics apps

An app is worth installing only if it covers a failure you'll plausibly hit. Below is the matrix as of late 2026, followed by an install-day checklist.

Two clarifications that trip people up:

  • "Offline" means different things. Google Translate's offline packs translate text and photos; Microsoft's packs support conversation; FlyTalk's packs play every phrase as pre-recorded audio with no model download at runtime. Check what actually survives airplane mode for your specific app.
  • Network reachability is a first-class criterion in China. An app that's excellent and unreachable is a party trick. Microsoft Translator and Baidu Translate are the two translation options that don't degrade the moment you land.

What each app prevents, in one line

  • Duolingo / HelloChinese / Memrise / Anki — prevent having no foundation at all; useful only with weeks of runway.
  • Pleco — prevents not being able to look anything up; keep it installed forever.
  • Google / Microsoft / Baidu Translate — prevent not being able to read; pick one that works where you're going.
  • FlyTalk — prevents freezing when someone answers you; the only one of the set built around the reply rather than the request.
  • Alipay / WeChat / DiDi / Trip.com — prevent the trip from stalling on logistics, which is where a phrase and an app get used in the same minute.

Install-day checklist

Before the flight, on hotel or home Wi-Fi:

  • Download your translation app's offline language packs (Microsoft for both text and conversation; Google only if you have a VPN plan).
  • Download the FlyTalk offline voice pack for Chinese; open one scene in flight mode to confirm audio plays.
  • Complete the tourist payment setup in Alipay with a foreign card before departure; test one small payment.
  • Install DiDi and Trip.com, and sign in once while you have signal.
  • Put the big-text emergency screen one tap away; find the pharmacy and allergen icons ahead of time.
  • Save two sentences you'll say most: "Please wait a moment" and "I don't understand — please speak slower."

If the trip is two weeks out, stop here: a course will not land in time, and the survival kit plus one offline translator covers nearly everything else. If you have three months, run Duolingo or HelloChinese for the foundation and still do this checklist — the checklist is about the trip going right, not about how much Chinese you know.
